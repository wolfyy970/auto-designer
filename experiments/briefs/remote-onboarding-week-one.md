# Remote new-hire onboarding, week one — design exploration

A new hire starts on Monday. They have never met anyone on their team in person. The company has overnighted a laptop, a swag pack, and a stack of PDFs. Their calendar has been filled in by HR: nine meetings on Monday, six on Tuesday, the rest of the week unstructured. Their manager has good intentions but is in a different timezone. There is no shared room to overhear, no kitchen to wander into, no one to lean over and ask the small questions: how do I VPN into staging, who is the actual person I should ask about benefits, is it normal that I still don't have access to Notion. The five-day window between joining and feeling like they belong is determinative — Gusto's onboarding research and similar studies link strong early onboarding to retention and time-to-productivity.

The user is a competent professional joining a remote-first company. They have done this before — they know how to set up a laptop and learn a new codebase or product. What they don't know is this team, this company's specific norms, this manager's actual preferences, and the political map of who-knows-what. They are exhausted by the meetings, anxious about not asking questions that would make them look new, and worried that asking how the company really works will mark them as not-yet-onboard. The remote format takes the natural social affordances of being on site — the watercooler, the lunch tag-along, the overheard standup — and replaces them with calendar invites, which are not the same thing.

We are exploring what week-one onboarding could be when designed for the remote new hire's experience rather than for HR's checklist. The scope is the first five working days, from Monday morning to Friday afternoon. The shape of the solution is open: it could be a structured set of guided experiences, an asynchronous companion that adapts to where the new hire is, a "buddy" matching layer over Slack, a redesigned company-wiki onboarding path, a calendar/schedule restructuring, or something else. What we want are distinct strategic directions for what week one could feel like.
