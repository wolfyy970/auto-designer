The design must complete the registration update before the next election the voter intends to vote in, with margin for state-specific deadlines. The failure mode to avoid: a successful-feeling completion that arrives at the registrar after the deadline.

The design must produce a registration update that will actually be processed correctly — the right form, the right documentation, the right address. The failure mode to avoid: an update submitted that is later rejected for missing or wrong documentation, with the voter discovering only at the polling place.

The design must work across state-specific rules without exposing the voter to that variance as their first impression. The failure mode to avoid: generic guidance that's wrong for the voter's state.

The design must respect that the user is in a life transition that has its own emotional weight (marriage, divorce, gender transition) and is already navigating a long list of bureaucratic tasks. The failure mode to avoid: making this task feel heavy when it should be light.

The design must coordinate sensibly with other name-change tasks (driver's license, Social Security, employer records) without overstepping its scope.
