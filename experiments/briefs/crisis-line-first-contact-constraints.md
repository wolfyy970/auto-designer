SAMHSA program requirements govern 988 operation. State-level crisis-center networks must comply with SAMHSA standards. Any tool that interfaces with 988 must respect those rules and the counselor protocols.

HIPAA and state mental-health confidentiality law applies to records of crisis contacts. State variation is significant; some states have stronger protections than the federal floor. The design must not produce records that violate confidentiality or create discoverable trails the user did not consent to.

Clinical responsibility cannot be replaced by software. A crisis counselor's judgment about lethality, intent, and next steps is irreducibly human; the design must not pretend otherwise. The role of the design is to make the right human conversation possible, not to substitute for it.

Accessibility is foundational. The audience includes deaf and hard-of-hearing users (TTY/TDD relay required; ASL via video is increasingly available), users with cognitive disabilities, and users with low literacy in the language of the service. WCAG 2.2 AA is a floor; the audience pushes the standard higher.

Language access is non-negotiable. Spanish at minimum for US implementation; additional languages where the local crisis center supports them.

Trust is the binding constraint. A single misstep — disclosed information that shouldn't have been disclosed, a police visit the user feared and didn't get warned about, a routing that produced a counselor mismatch — has consequences not just for the individual user but for the broader audience's willingness to reach out.

The design must work across phone, text, chat, and likely future channels (video, in-app), with consistent experience and consistent rules across all. The user does not always know which channel they need.
