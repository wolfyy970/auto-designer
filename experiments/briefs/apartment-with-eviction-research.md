The Eviction Lab at Princeton publishes academic research, court-records data, and policy analyses documenting how eviction filings function in the United States rental market. Their finding: an eviction filing — regardless of whether the case was resolved in the tenant's favor, dismissed, or even filed in error — creates a "scarlet E" that follows the tenant. Of the 3.6 million eviction court records the Lab tracked from 2011 to 2015, more than one in five contained little information on the case's resolution, but landlords' screening tools commonly do not distinguish between a filing and a completed eviction-for-cause.

PolicyLink, Shelterforce, and Stateline reporting have documented the structure of the tenant-screening industry: roughly 90 percent of landlords use third-party screening tools that bundle rental history, credit, employment, income, and criminal background into a simplified score or recommendation. The tools are minimally regulated relative to credit bureaus and operate with documented inaccuracies. Texas Housers and similar local research organizations have documented patterns of automation bias, where landlords defer to the score the screening tool produces and do not investigate context.

The user is the previously-evicted renter. They are not a monolith: someone evicted after job loss is in different circumstances than someone evicted after a domestic-violence incident, or after a landlord-improvement dispute, or in error. Eviction Lab research has documented disproportionate impact on Black and Latinx households, especially Black and Latinx women — patterns rooted in the racial dynamics of US housing markets. The renter may have rebuilt their finances and life since the eviction, may have references, may have a job — and still face categorical exclusion at the screening stage.

Federal Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) rights apply to consumer reports, but tenant-screening reports occupy a regulatory gray area; the FTC and CFPB have published guidance and enforcement actions in recent years documenting failures by the industry.
