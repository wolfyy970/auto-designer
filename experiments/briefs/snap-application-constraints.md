The application is federally regulated. SNAP is administered under USDA rules and varies by state, but federal law sets minimum standards for what the application must collect (income, household composition, expenses, citizenship status, identity), what timeframes apply (30 days for non-expedited determinations, 7 days for expedited), and what notices the applicant is entitled to. Any redesign must produce or feed into a submission the state eligibility system accepts. Regulatory compliance is a hard constraint; we cannot redesign the underlying federal rules.

Working memory and attention are compromised in poverty-related stress. Research on scarcity (Mullainathan and Shafir, "Scarcity," 2013) and related cognitive-load findings establishes that financial precarity reduces effective cognitive bandwidth in ways comparable to a night without sleep. The design must keep visible options small, avoid multi-screen sequences that demand the applicant carry information across pages, and externalize state at every transition. Limit visible options to 4–6 at any decision point; never require the applicant to remember a piece of information across screens.

The audience includes people for whom English is a second language and people whose primary language is not represented in the form. Federal civil-rights law (Title VI) requires meaningful access for limited-English-proficiency populations. The design must support translation, preferably with linguist-reviewed (not machine-only) renderings of the core text and with a path to a live human assistant in the user's language. Audio and read-aloud must be available for low-literacy users.

The design must meet WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility standards. The audience includes older adults, people with cognitive disabilities, people with visual or motor disabilities, and people using assistive technology. Color contrast, focus order, screen-reader compatibility, captioning of any video content, and keyboard-only navigation are non-negotiable.

Trust is a foundational requirement, not a feature. The design must not store applicant data beyond what is required, must not share data with other agencies for purposes the applicant did not consent to, must not use applicant data for analytics that imply surveillance, and must be transparent about what is shared with the state and why. Public-charge concerns for mixed-status immigrant families are real and historically grounded; the design must address them rather than ignore them.

The design must produce predictable response latencies on commodity hardware over variable networks. Beyond approximately three seconds per interaction, applicants in stress states often abandon. The architecture must allow the experience to degrade gracefully when network conditions are poor (offline-first capture, resumable sessions, SMS fallback) rather than failing closed.
