Target surfaces: responsive-web. The executor is doing this work across multiple devices over months — laptop during evenings at home, phone for quick lookups during the day, occasionally tablet. The administrative work itself (forms, document uploads, comparison across institutions) benefits from larger viewport; the emotional check-ins and creditor-interaction guidance work well on mobile web. No mobile-native app — the install friction is wrong for someone using the tool for the first time during grief.

State probate law varies materially. Informal-estate thresholds differ; community-property vs common-law marital-property states handle spousal accounts differently; some states require formal probate even for modest estates while others let nearly everything pass informally. The architecture must hold state-specific logic; producing generically-correct guidance is a hard constraint and a documented failure mode of every encyclopedic resource that exists.

Federal consumer-protection law (FDCPA) governs what debt collectors may say to survivors. The design must surface accurate guidance on permissible vs impermissible collector conduct and provide language survivors can use in response. Mis-stating the law is a hard constraint to avoid; under-stating it leaves the user vulnerable; over-stating it can chill legitimate debt resolution.

HIPAA controls survivor access to the deceased's protected health information. After death, the deceased's PHI remains protected; designated personal representatives have specific access rights under 45 CFR 164.502(g). Access for paying medical bills is workable but requires the right authority documentation. Any feature that involves accessing or sharing medical records must respect HIPAA's framework.

The audience skews older — many executors are themselves 50–70, the generation managing parents' affairs — and emotionally activated. WCAG 2.2 AA is a floor. Type size, contrast, and the ability to use the experience while distracted, exhausted, or in low-frequency engagement (returning after a two-week absence) must shape the design. Cognitive-load constraints comparable to those documented in grief literature apply.

The deceased's digital legacy is fragmented across providers with inconsistent (and frequently changing) terms-of-service rules for account access after death. Apple's Digital Legacy, Google's Inactive Account Manager, and various social-media memorialization rules each have their own paths. The design must keep current with these — out-of-date guidance produces frustration when the user follows it and finds the provider has changed the rules.

Trust is foundational. The executor is handling a deceased relative's sensitive financial life; the user must be confident that the system is not surveilling, selling data, or routing them into financial products. Independence from any single financial institution is likely required for the design to be trustworthy.
