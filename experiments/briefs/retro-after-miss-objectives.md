The design should make space in the retro flow for input that the team would not give if speaking aloud to leadership-in-the-room — without framing it as "anonymous mode" in a way that signals to leadership that hidden criticism is happening. The design move is to structure the conversation so the safe channel is the default for everyone, not an opt-in for the dissenters. The failure mode to avoid: anonymity built so heavily into the design that the conversation loses the named-author accountability that makes follow-through stick.

The design should surface what is happening across retros, not just inside this one. The design move is to make pattern recognition visible at the moment of the meeting — what the team flagged last retro, what was promised, what was carried forward. The failure mode to avoid: a longitudinal history dense enough to feel like a performance review, where surfacing a long-standing pattern reads as building a case rather than helping the team see itself.

The design should let the facilitator move the conversation rather than tend the tool. The design move is to keep the facilitator's hands free during the meeting — minimal admin overhead, no need to context-switch into formatting or board-management during the conversation itself. The failure mode to avoid: a facilitation surface so streamlined that the facilitator loses the flexibility to follow where the conversation actually goes, breaking out of the structured format when the moment calls for it.

The design should respect that retro content is sensitive — that the team would not want their unedited input visible to leadership, to HR, to a future hiring manager. The design move is to make the boundary between what is captured for the team and what is shared upward visible to the team at the moment of capture, before anything is said. The failure mode to avoid: a surveillance posture in service of trust, where the design's discussion of confidentiality so dominates the experience that the conversation never happens.

The design should hold for remote, hybrid, and co-located teams without breaking. The design move is to keep the conversation's structure stable across configurations — what the team does in each format is the same shape, even if the tools serving each are different. The failure mode to avoid: a single design optimized for one configuration that degrades sharply in the others, leaving the teams in the off-target configuration with a worse experience than they had before.
