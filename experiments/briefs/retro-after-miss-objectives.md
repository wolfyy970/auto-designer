The design must produce action items the team actually does before the next retro. The failure mode to avoid: a retro that surfaces problems and produces no behavior change, eroding the team's trust that retros are worth doing.

The design must support honesty without enabling blame-cycling. The failure mode to avoid: an experience that mistakes loudness for honesty, or that mistakes silence for agreement.

The design must accommodate leadership-in-the-room dynamics without forcing the facilitator to choose between honesty and political safety. The failure mode to avoid: a retro architecture that puts engineering managers in an impossible position.

The design must surface patterns across retros — what we keep promising to fix, what we keep missing — so the team can see the system rather than just the sprint. The failure mode to avoid: a tool that produces one retro at a time without memory.

The design must support remote, hybrid, and co-located teams. The failure mode to avoid: an experience optimized for one configuration that breaks the other.
