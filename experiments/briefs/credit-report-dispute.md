# Disputing a credit-report error — design exploration

A person has been denied an apartment, a job, a loan, or an insurance rate because of something on their credit report — and the something is wrong. A debt they paid years ago. An account opened in their name fraudulently. Identity confusion with a relative who shares their name. They have proof — receipts, closure letters, identity-theft affidavits — and no clear path to get it to the right place.

They face three different dispute interfaces, one per bureau, each demanding documentation in slightly different formats. Each often refuses to investigate unless the consumer provides items beyond what the law actually requires. The consumer is unsure whether to write a paper letter, use the online portals, or file a complaint with the CFPB. The process unfolds over thirty to ninety days; in many cases the original error reappears later from a different furnisher and the consumer is back where they started. They are exercising a right they have under federal law against three private companies whose dispute workflow is designed to deflect.

The scope is from the moment someone notices an error through the resolution of the dispute.
