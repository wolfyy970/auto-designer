# Disputing a credit-report error — design exploration

A person has been denied an apartment, a job, a loan, or an insurance rate because of something on their credit report — and the something is wrong. Maybe it's a debt that was paid years ago. Maybe it's an account that was opened in their name fraudulently. Maybe it's identity confusion with a relative who shares their name. The Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) gives the consumer the right to dispute, and requires the credit-reporting agencies (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) and furnishers to conduct a "reasonable investigation." CFPB data: between January and September 2021, the bureau received more than 500,000 complaints about credit reporting; the top issue, three years running, has been "Incorrect information on your report." Consumer-advocate analysis: agencies often conduct what amounts to a token review, converting the dispute into a two- or three-digit code and forwarding it to the furnisher.

The user is the wronged consumer. They have proof — receipts, account-closure letters, identity-theft affidavits — but no clear path to get it to the right place. They are facing three different dispute interfaces (one per bureau), each demanding documentation in slightly different formats, often refusing to investigate unless the consumer provides items beyond what the law requires. They are unsure whether to write a paper letter (recommended by NCLC for legally-significant disputes), use the bureaus' online portals, or file a complaint with the CFPB. The dispute process unfolds over 30 to 90 days; in many cases the original error reappears on the report later from a different furnisher.

We are exploring what disputing a credit-report error could feel like when designed for the consumer. The scope is from the moment someone notices an error through the resolution of the dispute. The shape of the solution is open: a consumer-side advocate that drafts and tracks disputes, a documentation packaging layer, an integration with CFPB complaint workflow, a coaching layer that helps the consumer understand their FCRA rights, or something else. We want strategic directions.
