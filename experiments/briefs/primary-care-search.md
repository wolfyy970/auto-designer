# Finding a primary care doctor after relocating — design exploration

A person has moved to a new city. They need a primary care doctor. They have insurance and a list of "in-network" providers that runs to several hundred names. The list does not say which doctors accept new patients, which have an opening within three months, whether they speak the patient's language, whether their office is accessible, or whether their patients feel respected. The patient calls one office, navigates a phone tree, holds for ten minutes, and is told the next opening is in five months — or that the doctor isn't accepting new patients at all. Multiply by ten calls, twenty calls.

People with an active condition — chronic pain, diabetes, anxiety — need to start care now and can't. People establishing care give up and only re-engage when something is wrong. The supply problem is real, but the experience layered on top of it makes the supply problem worse than it has to be: the directory hides exactly the information that would let a patient route themselves usefully.

The scope is from "I need a new doctor" through "I have an appointment booked." We want strategic directions that center the patient instead of the directory.
