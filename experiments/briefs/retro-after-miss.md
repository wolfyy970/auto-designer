# A sprint retro after a missed target — design exploration

A team has just finished a two-week sprint and missed what they committed to. The scrum master has forty-five minutes booked on Friday afternoon for the retrospective. The team is tired, frustrated, and uneven in how comfortable they are saying what they actually think — particularly with the PM in the room, particularly with leadership listening on the call.

The scrum master is the user. They want a real conversation about what went wrong and why. They want the team to leave with action items they will actually try. They do not want the meeting to become an airing of grievances against one engineer, a defensive performance for the PM, or forty-five minutes of "yeah it was tough." The retro tools they have are good at capturing sticky notes; they're not good at making the conversation honest, particularly when honesty has political cost.

A bad retro after a missed sprint compounds: the next sprint inherits the same patterns and the team becomes a little more cynical about whether retros help. A good one is rare and the team can feel the difference for weeks.

The scope is the retrospective meeting itself plus the prep and follow-through.
