ICU nurses doing shift-change handoffs are responsible for transferring complete clinical state about ~4 critical patients in ~30 minutes. Existing tools (paper SBAR sheets, scattered EHR screens, verbal-only handoff at the bedside) don't reliably surface the things that matter most at the moments they matter — pending lab results, recent vital trends, anticipated decisions in the next 12 hours, family context, what the outgoing nurse "has a feeling about" but can't fully articulate. Patient safety incidents cluster around shift changes specifically because handoff failures.

Design a handoff tool that respects the speed and gravity of this moment. The user is a critical-care nurse, mid-twelve-hour shift, who is either about to give report to the oncoming nurse or take it from the outgoing one. The tool sits in the bedside workflow — the nurse already has a scrub-pocket smartphone, a workstation-on-wheels with the EHR open, and 20-30 seconds of verbal exchange per patient.

The audience is professional, time-pressured, and operating in a high-consequence environment. They will reject anything that feels like a gimmick, anything that adds documentation burden without giving immediate value, and anything that pretends clinical judgment can be reduced to a checklist. They will respect a tool that respects their work.
