# The first 90 days after a Type 2 diabetes diagnosis — design exploration

A person has just been told by their doctor that they have Type 2 diabetes. The diagnosis is given in a 15-minute appointment along with a handout, a prescription for metformin or similar, and instructions to "watch your sugars" and "see a nutritionist." The Diabetes Self-management Education and Support (DSMES) consensus report — endorsed by the American Diabetes Association, the Association of Diabetes Care & Education Specialists, and several other professional bodies — recommends specific support at diagnosis. The reality on the ground: only about 6.8 percent of privately insured people with newly diagnosed Type 2 diabetes participate in DSMES within 12 months, and only about 5 percent of newly-diagnosed Medicare patients receive DSMES in the first year.

The user is the newly-diagnosed patient. They are scared, ashamed, confused about whether they "caused" this, and faced with a slate of changes (diet, exercise, medication adherence, glucose monitoring, regular labs) that nobody has helped them sequence. Published qualitative research reports patients describe needing: immediate emotional support for unexpressed concerns; information about available resources so they know when and where to seek help; and a connection with a health-care partner who serves as a guide through the transition. Generic apps that bolt on a "log your glucose" feature feel hollow because the patient knows the app doesn't understand what they're going through.

We are exploring what the first 90 days after diagnosis could feel like as a designed experience. The scope is from the moment the diagnosis is delivered through the 90-day mark, by which point the patient has either built habits or is sliding away from care. The shape of the solution is open: a phone-based companion that pairs the patient with a guide, a paced learning experience, an integrated layer between the patient's PCP, pharmacist and nutritionist, a partner-and-family-included experience, or something else. We want distinct strategic directions, not a better glucose tracker.
