# The first 90 days after a Type 2 diabetes diagnosis — design exploration

A person has just been told they have Type 2 diabetes. The diagnosis arrived in a fifteen-minute appointment with a handout, a prescription for metformin, and instructions to "watch your sugars" and "see a nutritionist." They walk out scared, ashamed, confused about whether they caused this, and faced with a slate of changes — diet, exercise, medication, glucose monitoring, regular labs — that nobody has helped them sequence. The professional consensus on what diagnosis-time support should look like is established; most newly-diagnosed patients receive none of it.

Generic apps that bolt on a "log your glucose" feature feel hollow because the patient knows the app doesn't understand what they're going through. They need a guide through the transition, not a tracker. The first 90 days is where habits form or don't. By the end of that window they have either built a workable rhythm or they have slid away from care, and the system will only catch them again at the next labs or the next ED visit.

The scope is from diagnosis through the 90-day mark.
