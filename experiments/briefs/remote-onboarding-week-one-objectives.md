The design should make the new hire's social map visible — who works on what, who to ask about which domain, who knows the codebase versus the customer versus the team's history — so the new hire can route questions to the right person rather than guessing. The failure mode to avoid: introducing the new hire to "the team" as an undifferentiated block during orientation, which produces names and faces without working understanding of who-knows-what.

The design should reveal the team's actual norms (not the stated ones) — how this manager actually prefers to be interrupted, what response time is real in this Slack channel, what the team's working hours actually look like once the European folks log off — through interactions the new hire can read directly rather than through a culture deck. The failure mode to avoid: a curated-norms document that the new hire dutifully reads on day one and then watches the actual behavior diverge from for the next month, eroding trust in the design itself.

The design should pace the first three days so the volume of new information (people, tools, vocabulary, context) does not exceed what the new hire can absorb, while still letting them feel like they're participating rather than waiting. The failure mode to avoid: optimizing for the manager's or HR's checklist completion, which produces a calendar so full the new hire has no time to read, think, or absorb, and walks into Wednesday already exhausted.

The design should give the manager a way to see where the new hire is stuck without making the new hire feel surveilled or graded — the loop is closed at the system level even when neither side performs a one-on-one well. The failure mode to avoid: a manager-facing dashboard the new hire knows about and performs for, eroding the honesty needed for the manager to actually help.

The design should create paths to the ambient context remote work removed — overheard conversations, casual side-channel asides, the working rhythm a colocated office made visible by accident — without requiring the new hire to schedule another meeting to find it. The failure mode to avoid: substituting more calendar invites for the missing context, which doubles the meeting count and reproduces the exhaustion the design exists to relieve.
