# Applying for food assistance — design exploration

Someone who needs food assistance now is sitting down to apply for SNAP. They've lost a job, had hours cut, gotten sick, or are raising a kid on inconsistent income. They are not the demographic the application was designed for. The form asks branching questions whose answers depend on context the form does not allow them to explain, requires documentation they can't easily produce, and is most often built to be filled out on a desktop computer they don't have. After they submit, the system goes quiet — they don't know if their application is being processed, lost, or rejected.

A meaningful share of people eligible for SNAP never connect to it. The application — not eligibility — is the structural barrier. The applicant carries that barrier alongside the rest of the stressors that brought them to needing food assistance: financial precarity, language, distrust of government processes, the exhaustion of being in scarcity. They get one chance to fail before quietly giving up.

We want distinct strategic directions for what a humane food-assistance application could be when designed around the applicant.
