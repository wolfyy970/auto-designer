# Pre-travel prescription preparation — design exploration

A person is preparing to travel internationally — for two weeks, two months, or longer. They take regular prescription medication: an SSRI, ADHD stimulant, blood thinner, daily inhaler, hormone therapy, controlled-substance pain medication. CDC and FDA guidance is clear in retrospect but invisible to most travelers prospectively: US prescriptions cannot be refilled by overseas pharmacies; many common US medications (including those for ADHD, pain, depression, allergies and decongestion) are illegal or restricted in destination countries; consequences for being caught with a restricted medication can include confiscation, denial of entry, or arrest. To carry enough supply for the trip, the patient needs a "vacation override" from their insurance, often requested weeks in advance, and may need a letter or certificate from their prescriber.

The user is two weeks out from a long trip and just realized they need to figure this out. They are using a mix of pharmacy phone trees, their insurer's app, the State Department's site, and Reddit travel threads. Their PCP's office is not optimized for travel-medication requests. Their pharmacy can fill the script if the doctor authorizes the override; the doctor can authorize the override if asked correctly. Nobody owns the work of getting this person across the world with their medication intact, legal, and in the right packaging.

We are exploring what pre-travel prescription preparation could be. The scope is the four-week window before international departure. The shape of the solution is open: a pharmacy-side travel-mode flow, a destination-country medication-legality lookup, a patient-side coordinator that talks to insurer + doctor + pharmacy + customs guidance, a paper documentation pack the pharmacist hands the patient, or something else. We want distinct strategic directions.
