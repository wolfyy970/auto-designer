HIPAA governs any handling of the patient's PHI, including glucose values, A1c results, medication lists, and clinical notes. Architectures that integrate with EHRs or pharmacy systems require BAAs and HIPAA-compliant data handling.

FDA software-as-a-medical-device boundaries apply if the design provides clinical decision support, dosing recommendations, or glucose-trend-driven alerts. Wellness-and-lifestyle guidance is generally outside FDA jurisdiction; clinical-action recommendation is generally within. The design must hold this line carefully.

Clinical content must be sourced from reputable bodies (ADA, CDC, NIH). The design cannot invent dietary or medication guidance and cannot present unproven approaches with the authority of established practice.

The audience includes patients with limited English proficiency, patients with low health literacy, patients in food-insecure households, patients in cultural contexts where traditional foods conflict with standard Western dietary advice. Language access is required; cultural adaptation of dietary guidance is required for the design to be useful for many patients.

Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA) is a floor. Audience skews older with associated vision and motor variability; type size, contrast, touch targets, and screen-reader compatibility must be appropriate.

The patient's clinical care is the system of record. The design must not become a competing source of truth that conflicts with the patient's chart. Where integration with the EHR is not available, the design must be explicit about what it does and does not know.
