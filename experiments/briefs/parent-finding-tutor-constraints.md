COPPA (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act) applies to any handling of data on children under 13. Parental consent, data minimization, and specific privacy rules govern any architecture that touches child data.

FERPA applies to educational records flowing from the school. Where the design integrates with school records, FERPA-compliant data handling is required.

State licensure and credentialing varies for tutors. Some states have specific rules for tutoring services (especially when subsidized through public funds or operating in school facilities); the design must hold this correctly.

Consumer-protection rules limit education-product claims. The FTC has taken action against tutoring services that over-claim outcomes; the design must not produce or amplify unfounded claims.

Equity is a binding constraint. The tutoring market disproportionately serves families with means; the design must not be an instrument that further widens the gap.

Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA) is a floor. The child and the parent may include people with disabilities; the experience must work with assistive technology and with families whose primary language is not English.

The architecture must respect the child's emotional state. Surfacing the child's struggles, grades, and assessments must be done with care for the child's privacy and dignity, even within the family.
