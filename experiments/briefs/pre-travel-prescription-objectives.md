The design must prevent travel-disruption incidents — confiscation, denial of entry, supply exhaustion mid-trip, legal exposure — for patients carrying legitimate prescriptions. The failure mode to avoid: an experience that gives the patient false confidence ("you're good!") for a destination where they are in fact at risk.

The design must produce, on time, the documentation patients need: filled prescriptions in correct quantity, letters from prescribers, certificates or apostilled documents where required, English-and-destination-language copies. The failure mode to avoid: a system that catalogs requirements without actually generating the materials.

The design must orchestrate across pharmacy, prescriber, and insurance, recognizing that the patient is the one bearing the cost of orchestration today. The failure mode to avoid: a tool that helps the patient know what to ask without helping them get it.

The design must respect that travel plans change; medications get lost; supplies run low. The failure mode to avoid: a one-time preparation tool that has no answer to the in-trip "I lost my pills in Buenos Aires" moment.

The design must work for the long tail of medications, not just the common ones. The failure mode to avoid: a system optimized for SSRIs and birth control that fails opaque for an obscure but legal medication.
