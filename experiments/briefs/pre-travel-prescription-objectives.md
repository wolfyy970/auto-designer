The design should produce, in time for the trip, the materials customs and pharmacies actually want — original-container packaging plans, prescriber letters on letterhead with NPI/DEA numbers, generic-name copies of prescriptions, destination-language translations where the destination requires them — rather than producing a checklist of what those materials should contain. The failure mode to avoid: a guide-style experience that catalogs what the traveler needs and leaves them to assemble it across the same fragmented systems they came in with.

The design should make destination-specific medication risk legible at first contact — which meds the traveler is carrying are restricted or banned at the destination, where the line is between confiscation and arrest, what documentation moves a controlled-substance medication from risky to acceptable — rather than burying the risk picture behind a generic readiness checklist. The failure mode to avoid: a confident "you're good" surface for a destination where the traveler is in fact at risk, or an over-cautious risk surface so alarming that the traveler abandons the legitimate medications they need to travel with.

The design should orchestrate across pharmacy, prescriber, and insurance rather than leaving the traveler as the integrator, while preserving the boundaries where each party must independently act. The failure mode to avoid: a tool that automates messages between parties so opaquely that when a prescriber declines to issue the letter or the insurance denies the vacation override, the traveler cannot tell why or what to do next.

The design should hold the medication long tail visibly — uncommon prescriptions, generics with different brand names abroad, recently-released medications without clean destination guidance, controlled substances at the most-restricted schedules. The failure mode to avoid: a polished experience for SSRIs, birth control, and ADHD stimulants that fails opaquely on the prescriptions outside its tested set, leaving the traveler with worse information than they would have had on the State Department website.

The design should account for the in-trip moments after preparation ends — meds lost in Buenos Aires, supply running short because the trip extended, an unexpected layover in a country with stricter rules than the destination — rather than treating "preparation complete" as the experience's end state. The failure mode to avoid: a one-time prep tool with no answer to mid-trip disruption, so the traveler in an emergency abandons the design and returns to phone trees and Reddit.
