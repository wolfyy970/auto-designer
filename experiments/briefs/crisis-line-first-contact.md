# First contact with 988, the crisis line — design exploration

Since 988 became the three-digit US crisis line in July 2022, more than 10 million contacts have been routed through it. Published evaluations report that 98 percent of callers say their crisis was de-escalated during the call; among one cohort surveyed, 88 percent said the call had stopped them from dying by suicide. Most callers are women and roughly three-quarters are 18–34. Some states have begun piloting "warm transfers" from 911 to 988 when the call is appropriate for mental-health response rather than law enforcement.

The user is reaching out — by call, by text, or by chat — for the first time. They may be experiencing a suicidal crisis, an acute panic episode, a substance-use crisis, or distress around grief, abuse, or identity. They may be the person in crisis; they may be a friend, family member, or co-worker trying to help someone else. They are reaching out at a moment of acute vulnerability. The first 60 seconds — what they encounter, whether they are met with judgment or warmth, whether they understand what is about to happen, whether they fear having police sent to their address — are determinative of whether they stay on the line.

We are exploring what first-contact with 988 could look like as a designed experience. The scope is the first contact (call/text/chat opening) plus the immediate next-step pathway. The shape of the solution is open: a redesigned web entry point, a redesigned chat interface, a "what to expect" preview surface, an after-contact follow-up companion, or something else. Out of scope: changes to the crisis-counselor protocol itself. What we want are strategic directions that take seriously how it actually feels to dial.
