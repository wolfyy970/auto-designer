Triage nurses in a busy urban ED scan a continuously updating list of patients waiting for assessment. The list refreshes as patients arrive and as upstream care teams update vitals or chief complaints. Most nurses do this scan multiple times per minute during peak hours.

(Inferred) The cognitive burden compounds late in shifts: nurses report that the visual rhythm of the list — equally weighted rows, color tags scattered uniformly across the page — stops surfacing the most urgent patient as separable from the merely waiting.

Critical signals nurses already use, in roughly priority order:
- Acuity level (1-5, with 1 being most critical)
- Time since arrival (long waits at high acuity)
- Vitals trend (worsening over time even at lower acuity)
- Chief complaint risk (chest pain, stroke symptoms, pediatric fever)

Nurses describe the existing interface as "I can find the answer, but it costs me." The cost is real: regulatory load, documentation burden, and emotional fatigue compound across a shift.
