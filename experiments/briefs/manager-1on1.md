# A tool for first-time managers preparing for 1:1s — design exploration

We're scoping a tool for new managers who need to prepare for one-on-one meetings with their reports. The space today splits roughly three ways: agenda templates and checklists (rigid, transactional, often abandoned after a few weeks), team-management software with 1:1 modules (heavy, coordinated with HR processes, not where new managers want to live), and notebooks or personal docs (works but provides no structure or support). New managers we've talked to describe a specific moment: 15-30 minutes before a 1:1 starts, they're scrambling between Slack, calendar, last-meeting notes, and the "what should I even ask?" anxiety. Existing tools either feel like compliance (someone else's framework) or feel like work (one more system to maintain).

Audience is people in their first 6-18 months as a manager — recent enough that the role still feels new, far enough in that they have multiple direct reports they're meeting with regularly. The reports they manage range widely (junior IC, senior IC, peer turned report, distributed team member, newcomer in their first 90 days). What new managers tell us they want is something that supports the specific moment of *preparing for the next 1:1* without becoming another tool that demands maintenance — they have enough of those already.

We're not committed yet to whether this is a single tightly-scoped surface, a short flow, or a standalone product. That's part of what design exploration should help us figure out. What we want from this round: distinct strategic directions for what "a tool for 1:1 prep" could meaningfully be — real bets that could move the category, not convergent variants of the same idea.
