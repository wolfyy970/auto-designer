Brex, Ramp, Concur, Emburse, and Payhawk product documentation describes the multi-currency expense pain in concrete terms. Brex's comparison documents, Concur's integration announcements, and Ramp's blog converge on the same picture: manual receipt capture, foreign-currency conversion that must match company policy, multi-currency cards generating fees and declines, and reconciliation requiring access to siloed systems.

The user is the business traveler. The audience spans consultants, sales engineers, executives, account managers, researchers, journalists, and any role where international travel is part of the work. Trip durations vary (overnight to multi-month deployments). Some travelers are reimbursed for personal cards; many use company cards (which create their own multi-currency frictions); a few use travel-management companies that pre-pay. The post-trip work is the same regardless: produce a report the finance team can approve.

The systems on the finance side are themselves users. Concur, Ramp, Brex, Emburse, and similar tools are built for finance-team workflows (policy enforcement, expense categorization, GL coding, audit, tax). Their UX often reflects this priority. (Inferred from the consistency of traveler-side complaints across all vendor blogs) the traveler experience is a tradeoff the finance-tool roadmap makes.

Modern expense tools (Brex, Ramp) automate more receipt capture than legacy tools (Concur, Emburse) — Brex's product marketing describes auto-uploaded itemized receipts for purchases; Ramp's blog describes capture-time policy enforcement. The friction remaining is at the long tail: tips paid in cash, receipts in languages the OCR doesn't handle well, FX-rate mismatches between card statements and policy rates, charges that hit multiple currencies (pre-auth in one, settlement in another), and the small-dollar receipts (taxis, coffee) that are required but disproportionately painful to capture.
