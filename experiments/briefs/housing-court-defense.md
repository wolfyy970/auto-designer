# Self-representation in housing court — design exploration

A tenant has received an eviction notice. Their landlord has filed in housing court. They have a court date in two to three weeks. They cannot afford an attorney; the public-interest legal aid organization in their county is at capacity. Existing self-help resources — published by civil legal aid clinics, state court self-help centers, and consumer-protection orgs — are accurate, thorough, and often unusable in this state: they are 40-page PDFs of guidance written in legalese for an exhausted person who is also working two jobs and has children at home. The Legal Aid of North Carolina Eviction Defense Manual is 200+ pages.

The user is the tenant. They may have a valid legal defense (the landlord did not give proper notice; the rent claim is incorrect; the landlord changed the locks or shut off utilities, which is unlawful self-help eviction; the unit is uninhabitable). Or they may not have a defense and need to negotiate time, settle, or file an answer to preserve options. They have a stack of papers in three different formats, a phone, and an internet connection. They cannot tell which of the 80 pieces of self-help advice apply to their specific facts and which don't.

We are exploring what a tool for this situation could be. The scope is from "I got a notice" through "I have prepared for court." The shape of the solution is open: a guided-interview tool that produces filed paperwork, a triage layer that helps the tenant identify defenses, a court-prep companion that walks them through what to expect, a hand-off to legal aid that arrives with the right pre-work done, or something else. The user is in real distress; nothing precious; what they need is to be able to walk into court understanding what is about to happen and what they should say.
