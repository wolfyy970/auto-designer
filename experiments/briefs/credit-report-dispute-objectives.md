The design must produce successful dispute resolutions for consumers with legitimate errors. The failure mode to avoid: an experience that helps the consumer through the motions without changing the outcome rate.

The design must respect the FCRA's specific procedural rules — the right to dispute, the documentation requirements, the bureau-vs-furnisher direct-dispute distinction (15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(a)(8)(D)), the right to add a consumer statement, the right to obtain free reports. The failure mode to avoid: guidance that misstates rights or undermines them.

The design must address the recurrence problem — the same error reappearing later from a different furnisher. The failure mode to avoid: optimizing for the single dispute while the underlying data flows reproduce the harm.

The design must escalate appropriately. When the bureau dispute process fails, the CFPB complaint, the state attorney general, and (in some cases) civil litigation are available remedies. The failure mode to avoid: leaving the consumer stuck at the bureau dispute level when they have remedies they could be using.

The design must respect the consumer's time and energy. The failure mode to avoid: an experience that costs the consumer more in cognitive load and time than the disputed harm does.
