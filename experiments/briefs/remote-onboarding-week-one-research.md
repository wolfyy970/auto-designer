Remote onboarding research from Gusto, Lattice, and other HR-focused organizations consistently identifies the first week as determinative for retention and time-to-productivity. Gusto's published onboarding guidance frames specific patterns — buddy assignments, intentional video introductions, calendar pacing, async learning paths — as practical interventions for the documented gap between "first day" and "feels like part of the team." Lattice's research connects effective onboarding to retention, performance-cycle outcomes, and manager confidence; both organizations publish frameworks practitioners cite as the working canon.

The user is the new hire, but the user-system is larger. The manager is a user (they are about to invest weeks of attention). The team is a user (they need to integrate the new person into the working rhythm). HR is a user (they need compliance and reporting). IT is a user (they need provisioning to land correctly). The experience the new hire has is the visible surface, but the work behind it is distributed; new-hire experience failures are usually system failures, not personal ones.

The most documented pain pattern: meeting overload during the first three days, followed by ambiguity in days four and five. Calendar invites accumulate from every direction (HR orientation, benefits enrollment, IT setup, manager 1:1, skip-level intro, "coffee chat" with each team member), creating a Monday that exhausts the new hire and leaves them with no time to actually start. By Wednesday the orientation rush slows; by Thursday or Friday, the new hire is often alone with the documentation and unsure who to ask what.

(Inferred from the consistency of "I felt like I had to fake belonging by week three" reports across multiple practitioner blogs) the absence of organic context — overheard conversations, casual lunches, watercooler asides — is the structural change remote-work made, and the calendar-invite-replacement approach does not actually substitute for it. The design space is whether new shapes (async-first, peer-network, ambient presence, intentional shadowing) can replicate what was lost without simply doubling the meeting count.
