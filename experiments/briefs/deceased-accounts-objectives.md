The design must surface the most time-sensitive tasks — death certificates ordered in correct quantity, accounts secured against unauthorized access, Social Security notification, mortgage servicer notification — before less-urgent ones, so the executor does not miss a window that has legal or financial consequences. The failure mode to avoid: an experience that comforts the executor by giving them a sense of progress while the irreversible decisions go unmade.

The design must protect survivors from misrepresented liability. When a debt collector calls, the executor (and the rest of the family) must be able to access clear, jurisdiction-specific guidance about what they are and are not responsible for. The failure mode to avoid: deferring the question or routing the user to "speak with an attorney" when they cannot reach one — the design should be able to handle a meaningful share of standard cases on its own and route the harder cases out.

The design must respect the pace of grief without letting administrative deadlines slip past. The executor should be able to step away, return weeks later, and resume without losing track. The failure mode to avoid: either nagging the user with urgency that compounds distress, or going silent in ways that allow critical deadlines to pass unnoticed.

The design must navigate state-by-state probate variance — informal estates that pass via small-estate affidavits versus full supervised probate, threshold dollar amounts, spousal-allowance rules, homestead exemptions — without exposing the executor to that variance as their first impression. The failure mode to avoid: a generic checklist that produces wrong guidance for the user's state, eroding trust the moment it's tested.

The design must avoid the two opposite failures simultaneously: information overload (the 80-item undifferentiated checklist) and over-simplification (the four-step flow that papers over real complexity). The right shape sits in between: the surface stays calm while the system underneath manages the complexity.
