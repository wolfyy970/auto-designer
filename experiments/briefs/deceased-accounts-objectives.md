The experience should sequence what is actually time-sensitive — death certificates ordered in correct quantity, accounts secured against unauthorized access, Social Security notified, mortgage servicer notified — ahead of the larger checklist, so the executor encounters the irreversible decisions first rather than encountering them buried among the inert ones. The failure mode to avoid: an interface that produces a sense of progress through the easier items while the time-bound decisions go unmade in the background.

When a debt collector contacts the survivor, the surface should put jurisdiction-specific guidance about what the survivor is and is not responsible for within immediate reach — on the moment of the call, not after a research session. The failure mode to avoid: deferring the question to "speak with an attorney" the survivor cannot reach in the moment, leaving them to settle the call on the collector's terms.

The experience should hold state across long absences — the executor steps away for two weeks and comes back to a surface that still knows where they left off, what is still outstanding, and what has become urgent in the interim. The failure mode to avoid: a state-holding pattern so dense that the return visit reads as a backlog inbox, making the executor feel further behind than when they left.

The guidance the surface produces should be state-specific to the estate — informal-estate threshold, community-property treatment, spousal-allowance rules, homestead exemptions — without exposing the executor to that variance as their first impression. The failure mode to avoid: a generic checklist that produces wrong guidance for the user's state, eroding trust the moment it is tested.

The surface should sit between the two opposing failures the existing landscape demonstrates: the encyclopedic eighty-item checklist that respects no human reading it, and the four-step summary that paves over the real complexity. The surface stays calm; the system underneath holds the complexity. The failure mode to avoid: hiding so much complexity that the surface loses credibility when the executor hits something the simple flow did not anticipate.
