The first surface the user encounters should disclose, before anything else is asked of them, what is about to happen and what is not — that the contact is confidential, what the counselor's role is, and that emergency dispatch is not automatic. The failure mode to avoid: a disclosure burden so dense that the user's first encounter feels like a legal screen rather than a person on the other end, undercutting the warmth the rest of the contact tries to produce.

Routing into the appropriate specialization (Veterans, LGBTQ+, Spanish, deaf and hard-of-hearing relay) should be visible to users who need it without forcing every user through a triage flow before they can reach a person. The failure mode to avoid: over-engineered branching that loses users during navigation, or routing prominent enough that users who don't need a specialization feel they are being sorted into a category before they can speak.

The counselor surface should carry the context the counselor needs to respond well — what channel the user arrived on, what the user has said in the opening exchange, whether the user has indicated they are calling on someone else's behalf — without surfacing prior-contact history in ways that read as surveillance to the user. The failure mode to avoid: a counselor view dense enough that the counselor reads the screen instead of listening to the person.

The opening exchange should signal active human presence quickly — that there is a person on the other end engaging with what the user has actually said. The failure mode to avoid: a counselor-presence cue that mistakes immediate verbal acknowledgement for actual listening, leaving users feeling processed rather than heard.

The end-of-contact handoff should leave the user with a next step they can act on without having to remember the call — a written record of what was discussed, a name or number to follow up with, or a low-friction way back into the service. The failure mode to avoid: a handoff so heavy with resources that the user closes it without using anything, the way a printed handout gets folded into a pocket and forgotten.
