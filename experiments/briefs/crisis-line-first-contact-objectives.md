The design must keep the person in crisis on the line long enough to be heard. The failure mode to avoid: an onboarding experience that produces hang-ups at the very moment connection matters most.

The design must communicate, in the first 30 seconds, what is about to happen and what is not (in particular, that the call/text/chat is confidential and that police are not automatically dispatched). The failure mode to avoid: silent ambiguity that the user fills in with their worst expectation.

The design must route the user to the right specialization where applicable — LGBTQ+ Lifeline for LGBTQ+ callers, Veterans Crisis Line for veterans, language-specific lines for non-English speakers — without making the user navigate a complex IVR before they can speak with someone. The failure mode to avoid: over-engineered triage that loses the user during the routing.

The design must support the counselor on the other end of the line. Counselors are themselves users; they need context that helps them respond well without overwhelming them. The failure mode to avoid: surfacing the user's prior contacts in ways that feel surveillance-like to the user or distract the counselor.

The design must support the user's next step. Many contacts end with the immediate crisis de-escalated but the underlying situation unchanged. The failure mode to avoid: a hand-off into a void; the user needs a way forward that doesn't require remembering everything during the call.
