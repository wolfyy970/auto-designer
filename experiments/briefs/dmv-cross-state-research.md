State DMV documentation across NY, TX, CA, NJ, CO, and other states describes the process for transferring an out-of-state license. Timeframes for required transfer vary substantially: California gives new residents 10 days, some other states allow 30, 60, or 90 days. Documentation requirements differ: most states require proof of identity, proof of residency, the out-of-state license, and Social Security card or other proof of SSN; some additionally require birth certificate (for REAL ID compliance), proof of legal presence (for non-citizens), or marriage certificate (for name-matching across documents). Some states waive the written and road tests for valid out-of-state licenses; some require either or both.

The REAL ID Act (federal, 2005, with extended implementation deadlines) overlays the state license process: an upgraded "REAL ID" license requires specific documentation and is required for federal-facility access and (currently) commercial-air-travel ID starting at deadlines that have moved multiple times. The transfer is the moment when many residents discover they need to upgrade to REAL ID, adding to the documentation burden.

The user is in the middle of a move, with all the cognitive load that move entails (new lease, new utilities, new job onboarding, school enrollment if children, mail forwarding, banking address changes, vehicle registration as separate from license). The driver's license transfer typically sits in week 2 or 3 of the move-week-by-week task list. (Inferred from the consistency of state-DMV FAQ patterns) the user typically discovers required documentation upon arriving at the DMV office, having missed something — proof of address that's in their name, an unexpired Social Security card, an out-of-state license that hasn't been damaged.

Different audiences face different barriers. Naturalized citizens may need to show naturalization papers. Non-citizens with work or family visas need to show valid immigration documents and may face caps on license duration tied to visa expiration. Older drivers may face vision-test or knowledge-test requirements they haven't had to clear in decades. Transgender drivers may face the question of changing the gender marker, which interacts with state rules and federal documents (passport, Social Security card). All of these intersect with the basic transfer.
