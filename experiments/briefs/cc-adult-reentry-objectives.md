The design should give the learner a working picture of what their next eight weeks at this institution actually look like — the dates, the deadlines, the offices, the people, the artifacts they will encounter — at the level of specificity the institution actually operates. Generic college guidance is recognizable to the learner as not-about-them; institution-specific surfaces are. The failure mode to avoid: a procedural manual so dense it duplicates the college website it was supposed to translate, or one so abstracted from this institution that the learner has to re-resolve every step in the actual system anyway.

The design should let the learner pick up where they left off across days that include work shifts, school pickups, and irregular availability — sessions sized to the time the learner actually has, rather than to a continuous orientation flow. The failure mode to avoid: an experience that requires uninterrupted attention or assumes the learner has been thinking about it since they last opened it, leaving them perpetually re-entering the same step.

The design should make the human relationships the experience touches feel reachable rather than performed. Advisors, financial-aid staff, faculty, peers, and other adult learners exist; the design helps the learner cross the gap to them and recognize when a conversation rather than a form is the right next step. The failure mode to avoid: an automated companion so capable that it becomes a substitute for the human relationships the learner needs, or so insistent on connection that it forces interactions the learner is not ready for.

The design should treat the learner as someone who already has competence — work experience, parenting experience, language fluency, prior credits, prior college attempts — rather than as a blank slate. Where the learner brings something, the experience recognizes it. The failure mode to avoid: an onboarding that re-credentials the learner against an 18-year-old default, or one that defers so aggressively to claimed competence that learners who do need foundational support miss it.

The design should hold the friction the learner is actually meeting — financial aid that hasn't disbursed, a closed registration window, an advisor who hasn't replied, a course that's full — as a thing the experience helps them work through, not a thing it abstracts over. The failure mode to avoid: a polished surface that goes silent or generic the moment the learner runs into the actual constraints of the institution.
