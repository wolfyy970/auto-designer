The Fair Housing Act prohibits discrimination on the basis of race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, or disability. State and local fair-housing laws often extend protections (source of income, sexual orientation, gender identity, age, criminal history). Any tool that surfaces tenant information to landlords must not become an instrument of unlawful discrimination, even indirectly through correlated proxies.

FCRA rights apply when consumer reports are involved. Renters have a right to dispute inaccuracies; the tools the design connects with must respect those rights. Where the design itself produces a consumer report (a constructed renter-profile from records), FCRA obligations attach.

State and local eviction-record laws vary in transformative ways. Some states have begun sealing eviction records under certain conditions; some require records to be expunged after a period; some require the outcome (not just the filing) to be disclosed. The design must hold this variance correctly.

Race and class disparities are structural, well-documented, and cannot be designed away — but the design must not amplify them. Risk-score patterns that correlate with race (eviction records themselves correlate with race) require careful handling so the design doesn't reproduce the harm.

The renter's data is sensitive. The design must not create a new surveillance surface on the renter that worsens their position with future landlords, employers, or government agencies. Data minimization and renter-controlled disclosure are likely requirements.

Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA) is non-negotiable. The audience includes renters with disabilities (a Fair Housing Act protected class) and renters with low literacy. Plain language and translated content (at minimum Spanish for US implementation) are required.
