# A first-time manager's first performance review — design exploration

A person who has been managing other people for less than a year sits down to write their first performance review of an employee. They have 9 weeks of notes — most of them mental — and a template handed down by HR with fifteen sections and a five-point rating scale. Gallup's research finds that traditional performance reviews and approaches to feedback are often so bad they make performance worse about a third of the time. Lattice's research notes that both the manager and the employee are highly anxious during the review conversation, often selectively listening at best. SHRM and others document that most people managers are not trained to assess employees, and many do not have the time the conversation deserves.

The user is the new manager. They have never done this before. The person they are reviewing is in some cases an old peer, in some cases someone they hired, in some cases someone with more years in the industry than they have. They are scared of doing harm: of giving feedback that lands wrong, of being too soft and ducking real issues, of being too critical and damaging the relationship. They are also navigating HR's calibration cycle, where their ratings will be compared with peer managers'. Existing tools (the review form, last cycle's text saved on Notion, performance-management software with prompts and templates) deliver information rather than support; the manager is alone with the form and the cursor at midnight.

We are exploring what writing this review could feel like as a designed experience. The scope is the act of writing the review — the hours between "I have to write this" and "I have a draft I'd send." The shape of the solution is open: a prompt-and-guide companion, an AI-assisted draft layer that surfaces the manager's own notes back to them, a coaching layer that asks the right questions in the right order, a peer-review-of-the-review surface, or something else. What we want are distinct strategic directions.
