The design must reduce the post-trip work for the traveler to a small fraction of what it is today. The failure mode to avoid: a finance-tool-perfect submission that takes the traveler four hours to produce.

The design must produce submissions the finance team can approve at first review. The failure mode to avoid: a submission that's a kickback away from approval but kicks back anyway because of category mismatches the traveler couldn't anticipate.

The design must handle the long tail honestly. The failure mode to avoid: a system optimized for the common cases that breaks predictably on cash tips, foreign-language receipts, FX-rate mismatches.

The design must work in-trip, not just post-trip. The failure mode to avoid: relying on the traveler to do the work after they're home and exhausted.

The design must respect company policy without becoming a policy-enforcement bully. The failure mode to avoid: an experience that reads as an audit posture rather than a partnership.
