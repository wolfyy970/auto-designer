The design should make the traveler's post-trip session feel like reviewing-and-confirming rather than reconstructing — receipts surface in the order the traveler made them, in the categories most plausible given the trip, with FX-rate handling resolved in advance against the company's policy. The failure mode to avoid: a "smart pre-fill" that confidently miscategorizes and traps the traveler into accepting wrong defaults because the rejection-then-correction loop is the friction the design exists to remove.

The design should hold the long tail visibly rather than relegating it to a "manual entry" sidebar. Cash tips, foreign-language receipts, currency mismatches between pre-auth and settlement, and the small-dollar receipts the traveler may or may not have captured should appear in the same flow as the well-trodden cases. The failure mode to avoid: a polished primary path that breaks predictably on the cases that produced the multi-evening rework in the first place, returning the traveler to the original tool's failure mode through a prettier interface.

The design should make the traveler's policy questions answerable in the moment they arise — was the upgraded flight reimbursable, what counts as a client meal, how does the offsite gym charge get treated — rather than routing them to a separate policy doc the traveler cannot read on a Saturday. The failure mode to avoid: a chat-with-finance-team channel that's empty on weekends, which moves the friction from "the tool" to "the tool plus a four-day wait."

The design should make finance's side of the loop visible to the traveler at submission time — what category fits, what FX-rate will be applied, what flags will fire on review — so the traveler can see what the reviewer will see before sending. The failure mode to avoid: a finance-facing review surface so detailed that submission becomes its own audit posture, recreating the policy-enforcer-vs-traveler dynamic in the design itself.

The design should let the work happen in-trip rather than concentrating it post-trip. Capture, categorization, and policy questions should be reachable in the moments between flights and meetings, on the surface the traveler actually has in those moments. The failure mode to avoid: an in-trip capture experience so demanding that travelers skip it and revert to the post-trip reconstruction the design was supposed to replace.
