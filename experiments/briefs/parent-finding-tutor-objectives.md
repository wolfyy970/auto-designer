The design must connect the parent to tutoring intensity and quality that's evidence-aligned for the child's needs. The failure mode to avoid: optimizing for "match speed" while the matched intervention is not what works.

The design must respect the constraint mix: budget, schedule, child's emotional state, child's academic specifics. The failure mode to avoid: pushing a parent toward an HDT-aligned program they cannot afford or fit, leaving them with no plan at all.

The design must coordinate with the child's school where the school has services (Title I tutoring, learning specialist, IEP supports). The failure mode to avoid: an exclusively private-market routing that ignores public-school resources the family is entitled to.

The design must serve the child's experience, not just the parent's logistics. The failure mode to avoid: a tutoring fit that solves the academic deficit while breaking the child's confidence or motivation.

The design must work for the parent who is not herself a math expert. The failure mode to avoid: an experience that assumes the parent can evaluate a tutor's pedagogical fit on their own.
