SAMHSA's 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline is the United States' three-digit crisis number, replacing the 10-digit National Suicide Prevention Lifeline in July 2022. SAMHSA, FCC, and peer-reviewed evaluations (published via PMC and elsewhere) document the service's reach and outcomes: more than 10 million contacts since launch; in published research cohorts, 98 percent of callers reported their crisis was de-escalated during the call; among certain caller samples, 88 percent said the call had stopped them from dying by suicide. Callers skew female and 18–34, though the service reaches a wide range of populations.

Crisis Text Line, the independent text-based crisis service, has published similar research on first-contact patterns. Their documented findings include the dominance of mobile devices, the youth-skew of texters, and the predictive value of certain words. Both 988 and Crisis Text Line have addressed the fear of police involvement: the question "if I reach out, will the police come?" is a meaningful barrier, particularly for Black callers, trans callers, and callers with prior negative police-mental-health experiences. The actual "active rescue" rate is a small fraction of contacts, but the perception is structural.

The user is the person in crisis — or someone calling on their behalf. They are not in a state to navigate a long onboarding. The first moments are determinative: whether they encounter judgment or warmth, whether they understand what is about to happen, whether the system feels like it's for them. SAMHSA has invested in service-line specializations (the LGBTQ+ Lifeline Press 3 option, the Veterans Crisis Line connection, the chat and text channels) precisely because the audience is heterogeneous and first-contact tone needs to match.

(Inferred from the SAMHSA-and-pilot reporting on "warm transfers" from 911 to 988 in jurisdictions that have built the bridge) the architecture of how someone arrives at 988 — whether they dial it deliberately, are routed from 911, or follow a link from social media or a hotline aggregator — shapes the experience considerably. A user who has been routed from 911 has different context than a user who dialed knowing what they were doing.
