# Filing for unemployment insurance — design exploration

A person who has just lost their job is filing for unemployment insurance through their state's labor agency. They may have run through their savings or be close to it. They are filling out the application on a phone, in line at a job center, between caregiving shifts, late at night — and it is an application designed by lawyers, layered with anti-fraud questions in legalese, asking for information from pay stubs they may not have access to. A single wrong answer delays or denies the claim; the appeal that follows takes weeks they don't have.

Once they file, the system goes opaque. They don't know whether the claim is in process, what's owed in next steps, or when payment arrives — paper letters appear weeks later; the portal shows stale information; the phone line holds for hours. They are entitled to these benefits under state and federal law, but the application and the post-submission silence are the structural barriers between entitlement and money in the account.

The scope is the filing flow plus the first thirty days of claim-status communication.
