The design must shorten the time and cost of securing a stable home for a renter who has a past eviction. The failure mode to avoid: optimizing the renter's experience while structurally reinforcing the same screening-driven exclusion — a more comfortable journey to the same rejection.

The design must surface what factors are blocking specific applications, so the renter can contest inaccurate records, provide context, or focus on landlords who weight differently. The failure mode to avoid: producing an information asymmetry in the other direction (a renter who knows too much about how to game screening reports without addressing legitimate landlord concerns).

The design must respect that the experience reaches people in real distress — housing-insecure, sometimes already displaced, with children, with health conditions. The failure mode to avoid: gamifying or quantifying the rejection process in ways that compound the harm.

The design must work across the patchwork of state and local laws regulating tenant-screening practices and eviction-record availability. The failure mode to avoid: producing guidance correct in one jurisdiction and harmful in another.

The design must engage landlords as well as renters, given that landlord behavior is the binding constraint. The failure mode to avoid: a renter-only tool that polishes one side of an imbalanced relationship without changing the imbalance.
