The design should make legible to the renter what is on the screening report a landlord is seeing — which records, which inferences, which factor in the score or recommendation is driving the outcome on any specific application — at the moment the renter is choosing where to apply. The failure mode to avoid: an information experience that explains everything in the abstract but never tells the renter which line on which report is producing the decline on the listing they care about, leaving them as opaque to themselves as before.

The design should give the renter a usable path to act on what the report shows — to dispute records that are inaccurate, to attach context that fits the case, or to focus effort on landlords whose process weights the renter's situation differently. The failure mode to avoid: producing a path so detailed and procedural that only renters with weeks of time and high literacy can follow it, leaving the rest where they started.

The design should hold the renter as a person in housing instability, not as a portfolio of records being scored. Pacing, tone, and information density should fit a user who may be displaced, parenting through displacement, or under time pressure. The failure mode to avoid: a quantified rejection experience — dashboards of denied applications, score histories, rejection rates — that compounds harm by making the experience of being declined more legible than the experience of finding a home.

The design should hold variation across the renter audience without flattening it. A renter with a dismissed filing has different needs from one with a completed eviction-for-cause; a renter with documents in hand has different needs from one without; a renter who reads English fluently has different needs from one who does not. The failure mode to avoid: a single optimized path that serves the median renter and produces dead ends for the audience edges, which are where housing instability concentrates.

The design should leave landlords with a way to make decisions they recognize as well-grounded. Whatever new information surface the renter gains, it should reach landlords in a form their existing workflow can actually receive, so the renter's effort is not invisible to the decision-maker. The failure mode to avoid: a renter-side experience that produces context the landlord has no path to ingest, leaving the renter polished and still rejected.
