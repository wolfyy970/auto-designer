The design should let users move through the experience at their own pace, without progress bars, step-counters, or sequence prompts that imply a path to follow. Onboarding, content, and prompts should feel like options the user can take or ignore. The failure mode to avoid: a design so unstructured that users land on the homepage and don't know what is here or what to do with it.

The interface should demonstrate that the design knows what grief is — through copy that uses the right vocabulary, content that engages with specific loss types, and interactions calibrated to the user's stage rather than generic check-ins. The failure mode to avoid: copy that performs grief-literacy so heavily it feels theatrical, or that universalizes the experience in ways that exclude users with non-canonical losses (pregnancy, pet, anticipatory).

The experience should adapt to the loss type, stage, and support context the user brings, without front-loading a configuration wizard or asking the user to declare these things to enter. Differentiation among loss types, stages, and contexts should surface from the user's behavior over time rather than from upfront declaration. The failure mode to avoid: an opaque adaptation that the user cannot understand or correct when it's wrong about them.

The experience should feel like a private, low-arousal personal space rather than a feed, community, or engagement-driven product. It should not pursue session length, return frequency, or social sharing as measures of its own success. The failure mode to avoid: a design so withdrawn that users who do want connection or community have no pathway to it, or one so muted that the experience reads as cold rather than calm.

Pathways out — to professional grief support, to crisis services, to relevant communities — should be accessible to the user without requiring them to know what to search for or where to look. The failure mode to avoid: pathways so prominent they feel like the design is constantly assessing the user for distress, undermining the calm the rest of the design works to produce.
