# An adult learner re-entering community college — design exploration

An adult — 25, 35, 45, 55 — is enrolling at community college, often after years away from formal schooling. More than half of community college students are now 25 or older. The Community College Survey of Student Engagement (CCCSE) and partner research consistently surface the same barriers: lack of information about early processes such as registration, orientation, and how to make payments; institutional debts, textbook costs, and unmet needs blocking adult learners specifically; some students with an academic plan and supports, others with no plan and no felt support. Adult learners are also navigating jobs, caregiving, and the cognitive disjuncture of being asked to think like a student again.

The user is the adult learner. They have decided to re-enroll — perhaps for a new credential, an industry switch, an associate's degree, or developmental coursework before a four-year transfer. They have signed up but they have not yet started. They are trying to read the college's website, which is built for 18-year-olds applying for fall. They are unsure where to park, what to bring to orientation, whether the FAFSA they filled out applies to summer term, whether they can register part-time without losing the financial-aid bracket, and how to contact an advisor who responds in less than two weeks. They have a job that won't easily flex.

We are exploring what re-entry into community college could feel like as a designed experience. The scope is the eight-week window from "I've enrolled" through "I've successfully completed the first month of classes." The shape of the solution is open: a re-entry companion that adapts to the adult learner's actual life, a redesigned advising surface, a peer-network connector with other returning adults, an embedded text-based assistant, or something else. We want strategic directions.
