The design must produce an appointment with a PCP the patient can establish a relationship with. The failure mode to avoid: optimizing for clicks (search results, profile views) while the actual outcome — appointment booked, kept, and a relationship begun — remains unchanged.

The design must surface the information patients need at the moment of choice, including the information directories currently hide: accepting-new-patients status, real next-appointment timing, language and accessibility, scope of practice. The failure mode to avoid: presenting a confidently-curated list that's actually as opaque as the directory it replaces.

The design must respect that the patient is choosing a long-term care relationship, not transacting on a single service. The failure mode to avoid: an experience optimized for speed-to-booking that produces a poor match and a discontinued relationship by visit two or three.

The design must work for patients with chronic conditions, mental-health needs, women's-health needs, geriatric needs — patients whose match criteria are richer than "any PCP." The failure mode to avoid: a generic search that disadvantages patients with specific needs.

The design must respect the supply-side reality. There are not enough PCPs. The design cannot create capacity that doesn't exist, but it can route capacity better and surface alternatives (telehealth PCP, federally-qualified health centers, direct primary care, nurse-practitioner-led practices) where applicable.
