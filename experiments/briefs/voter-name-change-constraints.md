State election law sets the rules. The design must hold state-by-state variance accurately. Stale or wrong information has direct civic consequences.

The voter's personal data — name, address, date of birth — is sensitive and protected under state voter-registration confidentiality rules in many jurisdictions. The design must not exfiltrate voter data in ways that create new surveillance risks.

The design must respect the voter's privacy around the name-change event itself. For some users (trans voters, survivors of domestic violence), the old name may be deadname or unsafe to disclose. The design must not require the old name in ways that compromise the user.

Deadlines for registration-update vary by state (15 days, 30 days, election-day registration in some states). The design must hold accurate deadlines and surface them prominently for the user's state.

Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA) and language access are constraints. The audience includes voters whose primary language is not English, voters with disabilities, voters in low-income households without consistent internet access.

The design must respect that civic tech operates in an adversarial information environment. The design cannot become a vector for misinformation; it must keep its guidance impeccably accurate. Errors here have election-day consequences.
