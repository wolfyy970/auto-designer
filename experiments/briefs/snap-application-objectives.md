The design must lower the friction between "I am eligible" and "I have submitted a complete application." That means reducing the cognitive burden of the form itself (fewer ambiguous questions, fewer demands for documentation that the applicant cannot easily produce in the moment) and providing clear, calm communication at every step. The failure mode to avoid: a streamlined form that produces submissions the agency cannot adjudicate, increasing the time-to-determination on the state side and the chance of denial for incomplete information.

The design must surface the post-submission state clearly. Applicants need to know within minutes that their submission was received, within days what the agency needs from them next, and within the legally-required window (commonly 30 days for SNAP) the determination. The failure mode to avoid: false reassurance that the application is on track when state-side processing is in fact stalled — applicants need actionable transparency, not optimistic messaging.

The design must respect that applicants distrust government processes for valid historical and current reasons — particularly immigrant communities (mixed-status families, public-charge anxiety), people previously involved with the criminal-legal system, and people who have been denied benefits in the past. The failure mode to avoid: design choices that look reassuring to the agency's voice but read as coded data-collection or surveillance to the applicant.

The design must work on the device the applicant actually has — typically a phone, often a phone with intermittent data, sometimes a phone shared across a household. The failure mode to avoid: an experience that requires desktop, persistent internet, fluent English literacy, or document scanning that assumes a printer.

The design must produce a submission the eligibility worker on the state side can actually process. Eligibility workers, often under-resourced and constrained by federal compliance rules, are themselves users of the system. The failure mode to avoid: a redesign optimized for the applicant alone that creates downstream rework on the agency side, which slows determinations for every applicant.
