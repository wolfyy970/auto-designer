# Driver's license transfer after a cross-state move — design exploration

A person moves from one US state to another. The new state has its own driver's license process: timeframes range from 10 days in California to 90 days in some other states; documentation requirements vary; some states waive the knowledge and road tests for valid out-of-state licenses while others require both; payment methods accepted vary by state; appointment availability varies; in-person visits are usually required at least once. After the transfer, drivers receive a paper printout signed by the DMV worker as a temporary license while the hard copy arrives in the mail weeks later — creating uncertainty about whether the paper is enough for flying, banking, or other ID-dependent activities.

The user has just moved. They have rented a new apartment, are unpacking, are starting a new job, have a kid in a new school. They have a tab open with the new state's DMV site, but it's hard to tell which page applies to "transferring from out of state versus a new resident versus a non-citizen versus a recently-licensed teen." They have a stack of documents but aren't sure which they need: birth certificate, Social Security card, lease, utility bill, old license, marriage certificate. They want to know exactly when to go, what to bring, what to expect, and what to do if something goes wrong on the day. The DMV will not tell them ahead of time.

We are exploring what a driver's license transfer experience could be when designed around the mover. The scope is the four-to-six-week window after a move during which the license needs to transfer. The shape of the solution is open: a multi-state driver's-license-transfer companion, a state-specific guided checklist, a paper or app companion provided by the moving company, a chat-based DMV-prep assistant, or something else. What we want are strategic directions.
