# Updating voter registration after a name change — design exploration

A person has changed their legal name — after marriage, divorce, gender transition, or a personal choice. Their old name is on the voter roll. The next election is in five months. State rules differ widely: some allow online name-change updates; some require a paper form mailed to the county; some require an in-person visit with documents; some require re-registration entirely. Many voters do not know they need to update at all and discover the issue at the polling place when their name fails the poll-book check.

The user is the voter. They are mid-life-transition, with a long list of bureaucratic to-dos that the name change has triggered: driver's license, Social Security card, employer records, banking, passports, professional credentials. Voter registration is on the list, somewhere. They have heard from a friend that they should update it before the election; the state's election-board website is a tangle of pages and PDFs; they aren't sure which page applies to their county or their specific situation; TurboVote and similar non-profit tools help but are not perfectly tuned for the name-change scenario specifically.

We are exploring what updating voter registration could feel like when designed for this specific transition moment. The scope is the act of updating registration plus the surrounding ambiguity ("do I need to? when by? what proof?"). The shape of the solution is open: an SMS-first reminder companion, a guided web flow integrated with state systems, a paper companion mailed with the new driver's license, a layer on top of TurboVote's existing platform, or something else. What we want are strategic directions for the moment of updating registration when life has changed.
