The design must support the patient through the first 90 days, by which point self-management habits are forming or not. The failure mode to avoid: a feature-rich app that the patient abandons in week 3.

The design must respect the patient's emotional reality at diagnosis — fear, shame, confusion, grief, occasionally relief — and pace information accordingly. The failure mode to avoid: an info-dump on day one that the patient cannot absorb.

The design must coordinate sensibly with the patient's clinical care team (PCP, pharmacy, dietitian, CDCES). The failure mode to avoid: surfacing guidance that conflicts with the clinician's plan, eroding the relationship the patient most needs.

The design must work for the family / household. Dietary change is rarely a single-person change. The failure mode to avoid: an experience that treats the patient as isolated, missing the household dynamics that make or break sustained change.

The design must surface the resources the patient is entitled to: DSMES sessions (covered by Medicare and most private insurance), CDCES referrals, dietitian referrals, sometimes peer programs. The failure mode to avoid: making the patient navigate this themselves when the right routing matters most.
