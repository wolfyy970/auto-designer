# Multi-currency expense report submission — design exploration

A person returns from a business trip across three countries with a wad of receipts in different currencies — paper, PDF, photographed, emailed, and a few in a third-party app. They need to submit an expense report to their employer's finance team within the policy window (often 7–14 days). They are using their company's expense system: SAP Concur, Brex, Ramp, Emburse, or similar. Brex's product marketing — confirmed by Concur's own integration documentation and Ramp's blog — describes the pain in concrete terms: receipt capture is manual, foreign-currency conversion must match company policy, multi-currency cards generate fees and declines, and reconciliation requires accessing siloed systems.

The user is the traveler. They are home for two days before the next trip. They have receipts in five formats, photos of three meals in a foreign language, an airline charge that hit their card in USD but was billed in EUR, a hotel charge that was pre-authorized at one amount and posted at another, an Uber that auto-uploaded but mis-categorized as personal, and a tip in cash they paid because the card terminal was broken. They have policy questions (was the upgraded flight reimbursable? what counts as a client meal?) but no one to ask quickly. Finance will reject the submission for a single category mismatch and ask them to redo it.

We are exploring what expense reporting could feel like for the multi-currency traveler. The scope is from the moment a trip ends to the moment the report is approved. The shape of the solution is open: an in-trip capture experience that handles the work as it happens, an AI-assisted reconciliation layer over an existing tool, a policy-as-conversation experience, a calendar-and-card-data joiner that produces a draft report, or something else. We want strategic directions.
