FERPA governs student records and is a hard constraint. The architecture cannot expose records to third parties without consent.

Financial-aid law (Title IV under the Higher Education Act, FAFSA process, Pell Grants, state programs) constrains what financial-aid guidance the design can offer. Misguidance has real financial consequences for the student.

Institutional information must come from the institution's official sources. Out-of-date catalog data, stale deadline information, or contradictory guidance is worse than no guidance.

ADA Title II applies to community colleges that receive federal funding. The design must support students with disabilities — older returning students often have disabilities they didn't have at 18 (vision, hearing, mobility, cognitive). WCAG 2.2 AA is a floor.

Language access (Title VI) applies where students' primary language is not English. Spanish at minimum for US implementation; additional languages where institutional populations support them.

Cost is a binding constraint for many adult learners. The design cannot become an additional financial burden; if it carries a fee, the model must accommodate students with no disposable income.

Equity is a binding constraint. Adult learners include disproportionate numbers of first-generation college students, students of color, students from low-income backgrounds; the design must not become an instrument that selects against them.
