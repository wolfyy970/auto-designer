The design should meet the small-business owner where their books actually are — partial, inconsistent across the year, with a Q4 surge of transactions the owner has not categorized and a stack of receipts that exists in three places. The design move is to make progress possible from any starting point, not just from a clean ledger, and to surface what is unresolved without demanding that the owner first reconstruct twelve months of bookkeeping before the tool becomes useful. The failure mode to avoid: a tool that presumes the books are in order and silently produces wrong numbers when they aren't, leaving the owner more confident and less correct than they were before.

The design should treat the owner's filing posture as a single object across the year — quarterly estimated payments, year-end close, the actual return, and the post-filing relationship with the IRS — rather than as four disconnected interactions that re-ask for the same context each time. The design move is to keep one continuous thread of business state that all four touchpoints read from and write to. The failure mode to avoid: a year-end-only experience that abandons the owner between filings and forces them to recreate state every quarter, replicating the spreadsheet sprawl the design was meant to replace.

The design should make deduction surface honest — eligible deductions the owner can substantiate are claimed, and the design does not nudge the owner into claims their documentation cannot support if audited. The design move is to pair each deduction with what would be required to defend it, in plain language, at the moment the owner is deciding whether to claim it. The failure mode to avoid: a deduction-maximization frame that optimizes for refund-headline numbers and silently increases audit exposure, transferring risk from the platform to the owner under the guise of help.

The design should make the boundary between what the tool decides and what the owner decides legible — entity-classification choices, accountable-plan setups, S-corp reasonable-comp judgments, depreciation method elections — so the owner can see which calls were defaulted, which were inferred from their data, and which they explicitly made. The failure mode to avoid: an over-automated experience that returns a finished return whose underlying decisions the owner could not explain to an examiner or to a future accountant, leaving them unable to amend, defend, or carry the position forward.

The design should hold the handoff to a human professional as a first-class path rather than a fallback. Many small-business returns benefit from a CPA or EA at exactly the moments — entity changes, multi-state nexus, large equipment purchases, ownership changes — when a self-serve tool reaches the edge of its competence. The design move is to make those handoff moments visible in advance and to pass the work to the professional in a shape they can use. The failure mode to avoid: a self-serve flow that closes off the human path so completely that the owner discovers the limitation only after they have produced a return they cannot stand behind.

The design should fit the owner's actual working bandwidth — short sessions stolen from running the business, often on a phone, often interrupted, often at the late hours when the owner has time to think about taxes at all. State persists between sessions, the next concrete action is visible on every re-entry, and the experience does not require a multi-hour clear runway to make progress. The failure mode to avoid: a session-resilience pattern so accommodating of interruption that the owner cannot tell whether they have actually filed, paid, or merely saved a draft of something that looks filed.

The design should be honest about deadlines and the consequences of missing them — quarterly estimates, the March-15 S-corp date, the April-15 individual date, extension mechanics, and the late-payment-vs-late-filing distinction many owners conflate. The design move is to name what is owed by when, in the owner's situation specifically, and to make the cost of delay visible before it is incurred. The failure mode to avoid: a friendly tone that softens the deadline structure to the point that owners discover the penalties only on the notice, and a design that takes responsibility for reminding without taking responsibility for being correct about what the deadlines actually are.
