**Target surface: mobile-native or mobile-web on the scrub-pocket smartphone, paired with the workstation-on-wheels EHR display as the reference surface.** The brief locates the tool in the bedside workflow at the moment of handoff; the phone is the surface the nurse can hold one-handed at the bed while the WoW screen carries the EHR. Directions that propose a third surface (desktop-only, central station, wall display) or that require the nurse to switch primary attention to the WoW for the tool itself are not viable. Whether the phone surface is native or web is open for hypotheses to vary on; the constraint is that the nurse-facing surface is the phone they already carry.

Regulatory frameworks constitute deal-breakers in two specific ways. **HIPAA (45 CFR Parts 160 and 164)** governs any handoff record containing protected health information; directions that route PHI through consumer messaging surfaces, unmanaged personal devices outside an MDM-equivalent posture, or cloud surfaces without BAA coverage are non-viable regardless of design quality. **The Joint Commission's National Patient Safety Goal on hand-off communication (NPSG.02.03.01, formerly Standard PC.02.02.01)** requires a standardized approach with opportunity for questions; directions that replace verbal handoff entirely, or that produce an artifact the receiving nurse cannot interrogate in real time, do not survive accreditation review at any Joint Commission-accredited US hospital and so are non-viable for the institutional buyer. State nurse-practice acts add a further layer where electronic handoff artifacts intersect with documentation-of-care duty; for a multi-state product the design cannot assume uniform treatment.

Audience reach realities operate differently here than in a consumer-facing brief because the audience is licensed RNs, not the general public — but two reach filters still bind. First, glove-and-fluid-resistant interaction: a meaningful fraction of bedside interaction happens with gloved, possibly wet, possibly contaminated hands (CDC standard precautions in critical care). Directions that require fine-motor precision, multi-finger gestures, or hover states are not viable for the surface as actually used at the bedside. Second, the night-shift / fatigue-end-of-shift / variable-ambient-lighting context (Inferred from established critical-care nursing research on shift-end cognitive load and ICU ambient conditions): directions that assume rested attention, optimal lighting, or quiet acoustics fail to reach the use moment. Cognitive, institutional, and operational realities at the viability level. The 30-minute / four-patient budget is a hard ceiling, not a target: directions that require more than approximately 7 minutes of nurse-facing input per patient to compose a handoff are not viable, because the time-box is set by the shift schedule and the bedside reality (Inferred from the brief's stated 30/4 ratio and the verbal-exchange budget of 20–30 seconds per patient). Cognitive Load Theory and working-memory research (Sweller; Cowan on working-memory capacity) apply at the viability level: directions that require the outgoing nurse to recall and re-enter information already present in the EHR add documentation burden the brief explicitly forecloses, and so are non-viable. The institutional reality is that paper SBAR, the I-PASS framework (Starmer et al., NEJM 2014, on standardized resident handoff; nursing adaptations follow), and verbal bedside report coexist as the incumbent practice — directions that require the unit to abandon all three simultaneously are not viable change-management bets.

Hard data and access realities. The pending-labs / recent-vital-trends / anticipated-decisions content the brief names as the things-that-matter lives inside the EHR (Epic, Cerner/Oracle Health, Meditech are the dominant US ICU vendors); directions that depend on a unified cross-vendor real-time feed without an explicit FHIR R4 / HL7 v2 ADT-and-results integration plan do not exist as feasible bets at the institutional-procurement timescale. The "has a feeling about" content the brief specifically names is the inverse problem: it lives nowhere in structured data and cannot be retrieved by any integration.
