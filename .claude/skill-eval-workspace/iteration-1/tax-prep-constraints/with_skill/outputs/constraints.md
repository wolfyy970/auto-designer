**Target surface: responsive-web.** Small-business owners reach tax-prep tooling across desktop and mobile contexts — desktop for record reconciliation against bank/accounting exports, mobile for receipt capture and check-in during the work day — and they cannot be required to install a vendor app at the moment they engage with tax content. Native and PWA-installable extensions are downstream; the strategic surface declaration is responsive-web.

Regulatory frameworks constitute hard deal-breakers in this domain and are not negotiable through design quality. **IRC §7216** (Treasury Regulations on tax-return-preparer disclosure and use of taxpayer information) makes directions that reuse return data for marketing, cross-sale, or third-party analytics without specific written consent non-viable; the consent regime is structurally narrow and any direction that depends on ambient data reuse fails here. **Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA) Safeguards Rule**, enforced by the FTC against "financial institutions" — a category the FTC has interpreted to include tax preparers — imposes administrative, technical, and physical safeguards that filter out directions assuming consumer-grade data handling. The **IRS Free File Alliance agreement** (and its post-2021 reforms after the ProPublica reporting on TurboTax's Free File suppression) constrains any direction that positions itself as "free filing" against IRS partners; directions that conflate marketing-free with Free-File-program-free are non-viable. Substantive tax law lives in **IRC Title 26, IRS Pub 17 (for individuals filing Schedule C), and the Schedule C / SE / 1040-ES instructions**, with **state Department of Revenue** codes adding a non-uniform layer — a direction that assumes federal-only scope cannot reach sole proprietors who owe state income tax, state sales tax, or local business tax in the 45+ states with such regimes. Directions that try to abstract over state variation rather than confront it are not viable for the audience.

Audience reach realities filter the exploration space before design quality enters. The small-business-owner population is not the consumer-filer population: it disproportionately includes sole proprietors, gig workers, and microbusiness operators whose tax exposure is structurally more complex (Schedule C, SE tax, quarterly estimated payments, 1099-NEC reconciliation) than W-2 filers. (Inferred from BLS self-employment data and IRS SOI Schedule C filing volumes) a meaningful share of this audience operates in rural and low-broadband contexts, in English-as-second-language households, and with intermittent device access shared across a household or business. Directions that assume always-on broadband, single-device persistent sessions, or English-only literacy at IRS-form reading-level fail to reach the audience edges where small-business tax pain is most acute. The disability-accessibility-as-audience-reach version is real here: small-business owners include disproportionately many older operators and operators with vision, motor, or cognitive disabilities continuing to run businesses; directions that fundamentally exclude this composition are not viable. (Rendering-level accessibility specifics live in execution work, not here.)

Cognitive, institutional, and operational realities at the viability level. (Inferred from Cognitive Load Theory — Sweller — and Yerkes-Dodson on arousal and performance) Tax-prep work concentrates against a fixed external deadline (April 15 federal, varying state) and frequently against a cash-flow scarcity context — owners are doing this work in the same weeks they are managing payroll, AR, and operating decisions, with the IRS as a counterparty whose enforcement asymmetry is well understood by the user. Direction shapes that demand multi-session sustained engagement, that require the user to assemble a complete document set before any progress is possible, or that present the entire return as one undifferentiated form do not survive contact with the audience's actual time-and-attention budget. Institutional reality: tax preparers operate under Circular 230 and PTIN regimes; directions that position software as offering "tax advice" rather than tax-preparation support cross a regulated line and become non-viable without a credentialed-preparer layer the brief does not commit to. (How a chosen direction renders to accommodate these realities — progressive disclosure, save-and-resume, recognition-over-recall — belongs in execution.)

Hard data and access realities. The data a class of direction would need to fully automate small-business tax prep does not exist in unified form: there is no consolidated income API across the marketplaces, processors, and clients a sole proprietor transacts with; 1099-NEC issuance is fragmented and partial; bank/accounting data lives in formats that vary by institution and accounting tool; prior-year-return access requires either user-side document possession or IRS transcript retrieval through an authentication regime (IRS account creation via ID.me) that is itself a known friction point for the target audience. Directions that depend on a unified income-data layer, on guaranteed prior-year-return availability, or on frictionless IRS-side authentication do not exist as feasible directions. (Inferred) State Department of Revenue APIs and data-portability surfaces are even less uniform than federal; directions that assume programmatic state-side integration fail at the access layer.
