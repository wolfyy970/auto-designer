**Target surfaces: open across the tax-year cycle, but the surface choice must survive the audience's actual context.** A small-business owner's tax work is not a single sitting in front of a desktop browser the week before April 15 — it spans a year of bookkeeping decisions, a quarterly estimated-payment cadence, a several-week document-gathering phase, the return-preparation phase itself, and a post-filing exposure that may run six years for the records-retention obligation and longer for state returns. Directions are open to declare responsive-web, mobile-web, an SMS or messaging companion for the deadline-reminder and document-request beats, a desktop client where the keyboard-heavy data-entry work happens, an integration into the bookkeeping software the owner already uses (QuickBooks Online, Xero, Wave, FreshBooks), or a service-design shape that pairs the digital surface with a human preparer or enrolled agent. The constraint is that the surface declaration is named by the proposal, and that the declared surface does not assume the owner can sit at a single device for a continuous block — the audience's days are interrupt-driven by definition.

Regulatory frameworks constitute hard deal-breakers, and the tax domain has more of them than most. The Internal Revenue Code (Title 26 of the United States Code) and the implementing Treasury Regulations govern what a return must contain; IRS publications (Pub 334 for the small-business tax guide, Pub 535 for business expenses, Pub 583 on starting a business and keeping records, Pub 463 on travel and meals) define the practitioner-facing surface of those rules. Returns themselves are filed on entity-specific forms — Schedule C for sole proprietors and single-member LLCs taxed as disregarded entities, Form 1065 with Schedule K-1s for partnerships and multi-member LLCs, Form 1120-S with K-1s for S corporations, Form 1120 for C corporations — and the schedule a direction assumes the user is filing is not abstract: getting the entity classification wrong produces a return the IRS will reject or audit. Self-employment tax (Schedule SE), estimated quarterly payments (Form 1040-ES, with safe-harbor rules under §6654), the qualified business income deduction (§199A, codified by the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act with provisions sunsetting after 2025), depreciation regimes (§168 MACRS, §179 expensing, bonus depreciation under §168(k)), and the 1099-NEC / 1099-K information-return regime are all live surface area the return preparation interacts with. The IRS e-file program is governed by Publication 1345 for authorized e-file providers; any direction that proposes to actually transmit returns to the IRS must do so through an Electronic Return Originator and the Modernized e-File (MeF) system. State tax filings add a parallel obligation in 41 states plus DC for income tax and in every state for some combination of sales tax, payroll tax, franchise tax, and gross-receipts tax; directions that assume a uniform national filing collapse on contact with the state surface. Sales-tax nexus rules post-*Wayfair* (South Dakota v. Wayfair, 2018, 138 S. Ct. 2080) require multi-state sales-tax registration and filing for businesses meeting economic-nexus thresholds, and the threshold definitions are state-specific. Worker-classification rules (the federal common-law test, plus state-specific tests including California's ABC test under AB 5) bind the 1099-vs-W-2 question and are not a design choice. Beneficial-ownership information reporting under the Corporate Transparency Act (31 U.S.C. § 5336) imposes a parallel federal filing obligation on most small entities to FinCEN; its status is in active litigation but design directions cannot assume it has gone away. Practitioner-conduct rules — Treasury Department Circular 230 — bind anyone who practices before the IRS, and §6694 imposes preparer penalties on returns understating tax. A direction that produces a return the IRS will not accept, or that generates preparer-side liability the deploying entity cannot carry, is not viable regardless of design quality.

Trust posture and the rendering-of-advice question are themselves a binding viability constraint, not a UX preference. Tax answers are legal answers in disguise: telling a user that a specific expense is deductible, that a specific worker is a contractor, or that a specific transaction is a non-taxable distribution rather than wages is a legal conclusion with audit and penalty consequences. Unauthorized practice of law and unauthorized practice in front of the IRS (the §6694 surface, plus state CPA and attorney licensing boards) are not theoretical — directions that produce conclusory tax advice from an unlicensed automated layer expose the owner to the exam-and-penalty downside and the deploying party to enforcement. Some directions can absorb this with appropriate scoping (calculators, organizers, guided-data-entry that explicitly defers conclusions to the owner or to a human preparer); some directions cannot. Directions that depend on the system rendering a definitive answer to a fact-pattern the system cannot fully verify — and presenting that answer with the affordance of finality — are not viable on liability grounds even where they are nominally legal. Adjacent to this, the audience trust posture is itself a constraint: small-business owners' prior interactions with tax software ([Intuit's $141M FTC settlement on free-filing dark patterns](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/02/ftc-takes-action-against-intuit-its-deceptive-turbotax-its-free-free-free-ad-campaign)) and with IRS enforcement have produced a baseline of warranted suspicion; directions that surface the cost, the disclosure boundary, and the limits of what the system is doing are reach strategies the audience can absorb. Directions that obscure those boundaries are not.

Audience reach realities filter direction shapes severely, because "small-business owner" is not a single audience. The US Census County Business Patterns and SBA characterize the population as roughly 33 million small businesses, of which the great majority are sole proprietorships and non-employer entities; the entity mix runs from the sole-proprietor Schedule C filer with a thousand-dollar Square account through the multi-member partnership with K-1s and basis tracking through the S-corp owner-employee taking reasonable compensation and distributions through the C-corp owner managing accumulated earnings. The tax-knowledge baseline runs the full range from owners whose financial literacy is uneven and whose books are a shoebox or a Google Sheet, to owners with a bookkeeper and a CPA already in the workflow. The language baseline is not uniform either: the Hispanic-owned-business, immigrant-owned-business, and limited-English-proficient-owner segments are significant fractions of the small-business population, and Title VI plus state-level language access obligations apply to publicly-facing government touchpoints in this space. Owners with disabilities — cognitive, motor, vision — are within the audience this work must reach. Directions that assume a single archetype (the tech-fluent solo consultant on TurboTax Self-Employed; the QuickBooks-power-user with clean books) reach only a slice of the audience and underserve the segments where the harm of doing taxes badly concentrates. (Rendering-level accessibility and localization specifics live in execution work, not here.) The audience also varies in time-availability and back-office support: a one-person business is doing this work after closing, on the phone, between deliveries; the assumption that the owner can complete the work in a contiguous business-day block does not hold.

Cognitive and operational realities at the viability level. (Inferred from established research on the cognitive effects of scarcity — Mullainathan and Shafir, *Scarcity*, 2013 — and from Sweller's Cognitive Load Theory, plus tax-specific evidence in the Government Accountability Office and Taxpayer Advocate Service annual reports on small-business compliance burden) The small-business owner does not arrive at the tax interaction in a neutral state: they arrive in the financial and cognitive condition of running the business itself, often under cash-flow pressure, often catching up on bookkeeping that was deferred during the quarters where revenue was thin, often anticipating a tax bill they cannot easily pay. Tax anxiety is not a soft phenomenon — it is correlated with avoidance, with late-filing, with the failure-to-file and failure-to-deposit penalty regime under §6651 and §6656 that compounds the original problem. Direction shapes that require the owner to surface every transaction in a single session, that gate progress on a Form 1099 the owner has not yet received from a payer, that demand the owner make terminology-heavy classification decisions ("did you elect §179 expensing? what is your accumulated adjustments account balance?") without scaffolding, do not survive contact with the audience's actual condition. The preparer-side reality is parallel: where a direction includes a CPA or enrolled agent reviewer, that reviewer is operating under a compressed busy-season load (late January through April 15, with a second compression around the October 15 extended deadline), often handling several hundred returns per cycle. Directions that resolve owner-side friction by exporting unbounded rework to the preparer are not viable as deployed systems either.

Connectivity, resumability, and the multi-source-of-truth reality. Tax work is by nature multi-session and multi-source: the bookkeeping software is one source of truth, the bank and credit-card statements are another, the payroll provider (Gusto, ADP, Paychex, Justworks) is another, the merchant-acquirer 1099-K is another, the prior-year return is another, the receipt pile is another, the spouse's W-2 is another for a sole-prop filing jointly. Directions that depend on a single uninterrupted session, on a single device, or on the owner having all source documents in hand at the start, do not survive contact with reality. The application surface must hold the owner's progress across signal drops, across the weeks between when they start and when the last 1099 arrives, across devices, and across the return-preparer handoff where applicable. (The technical mechanics of resumability live in execution; the strategic constraint is that any direction must inhabit a multi-week, multi-source, partial-data reality from the start.)

Data and access realities. There is no unified federal API that returns a small-business owner's prior-year information, their open tax-account balances, their estimated-payment ledger, or their 1099s received; the IRS Online Account for Business is a partial surface, the Transcript Delivery Service requires e-Services credentialing the consumer-facing system cannot inherit, and state DOR portals are 50-plus separate integrations of varying maturity. Directions that depend on a unified-government-data plane do not exist as feasible. The bookkeeping-software integrations (QuickBooks Online API, Xero API, plus aggregators like Plaid for bank-level connectivity) are real but introduce a permissions, refresh-token, and data-quality surface the design must hold; the categorization the bookkeeping software has applied is not always the categorization that flows correctly to a Schedule C, and an undisclosed re-mapping silently changes the return. Payroll integrations are similarly heterogeneous. The 1099-K threshold under §6050W has been in active flux (the $600 threshold legislated by ARPA was repeatedly delayed by IRS notice and is currently phasing in at $5,000 for 2024, $2,500 for 2025, $600 from 2026 forward per IRS Notice 2024-85, subject to change), and directions that hard-code a threshold value will encode last year's law. PII and financial-data handling carries the same regulatory weight as in the other financial-domain briefs: SSNs and EINs (where the EIN is functionally the SSN for the entity), bank-account and routing numbers used for direct deposit of refunds or direct debit of payments, full transaction-level ledgers, IP-PIN credentials issued by the IRS for identity-verified taxpayers — directions that aggregate this into a new exposure surface, that share it with third parties beyond what the return requires (the FTC's 2023 actions against tax preparers for sharing taxpayer data with Meta and Google are the relevant landscape), or that fail to provide the §7216 disclosure-and-consent path for any use of return information beyond return preparation, are not viable.

Fraud-control and identity realities. Tax-related identity theft and refund fraud are a documented and ongoing problem (Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration annual reports; the IRS Identity Protection PIN program exists for this reason). Defenses against this — identity verification at account creation and at the point of e-filing, the IP-PIN integration where the taxpayer has one, fraud-detection holds the IRS applies post-submission — are not optional. Directions that strip identity verification to streamline the experience are not viable. The exploration remains open on how the controls are presented, sequenced, and softened for the audience; it is not open on whether they exist.

Deadline-binding reality, distinct from cognitive load. Tax has hard external deadlines the system cannot move: the April 15 individual and sole-prop deadline, the March 15 partnership and S-corp deadline, the quarterly estimated-payment dates (April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15), the October 15 extended deadline, plus state deadlines that do not always align. Failure to meet them produces statutory penalties under §6651 (failure to file, 5% per month up to 25%) and §6654 (estimated-tax underpayment) plus interest. A direction whose flow does not visibly and reliably get the owner to the deadline on time is not a viable direction, however good the experience preceding the deadline is. The mirror obligation is the records-retention horizon: the §6501 statute of limitations on assessment is generally three years from filing but extends to six years for a 25%-or-greater income omission and is unlimited for fraud; directions that do not produce records the owner can still access and reconstruct from at year six are not viable on the audit-survival ground.
