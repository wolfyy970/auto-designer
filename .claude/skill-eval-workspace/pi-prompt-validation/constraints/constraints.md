Surface is `mobile-native-ios` and `mobile-native-android` as the primary plane, with `mobile-web` and `responsive-web` as a parallel reach plane — the brief frames the user as locked out of "the banking app," but lockout almost always pushes the user off the credentialed app surface and into whatever channel is reachable from a locked state (a marketing site, a Google result, an SMS link, a help-search). A direction that lives exclusively inside the authenticated mobile app is not viable; the reset entry point has to be reachable from the un-authenticated outside. (Inferred from the brief's framing of "locked out" plus the explicit allowance for pre-auth landing and support-escalation surfaces.)

The load-bearing filter for this brief is the user's state, not the regulatory floor. The user is in acute financial-time pressure ("pay rent," "check a balance") while simultaneously being treated as a fraud suspect by the current system. Yerkes-Dodson applies directly: this is a high-arousal user whose performance on multi-step verification, recall of past credentials, or branching decision-trees degrades sharply. Direction shapes that assume a moderate-arousal user calmly working through a recovery flow do not survive contact. Mullainathan-Shafir scarcity bandwidth tax (Mullainathan & Shafir, *Scarcity*, 2013) compounds this for the rent-due cohort — cognitive bandwidth is already consumed by the precipitating event, so directions that demand sustained attention or context-reconstruction across interruptions filter out. The viability test for any direction is: does it function for a user who is angry, time-pressured, and cognitively taxed before the flow even starts?

Regulatory and fraud-control realities set a real ceiling on how aggressively a direction can compress verification. Banking app authentication sits inside U.S. financial-services identity-proofing expectations (FFIEC authentication guidance for online banking, GLBA safeguarding of customer information, BSA/AML KYC re-verification triggers, and state-level breach-notification regimes) plus card-network and ACH fraud-loss exposure that the institution — not the user — eats when reset becomes an account-takeover vector. Directions that propose "frictionless" resets without a credible identity-binding step are not viable regardless of UX quality; the bank cannot ship them. Directions that propose offloading identity proofing onto a third-party IdP, a device-bound passkey, or a previously-trusted device are viable shapes; directions that propose removing the proof step entirely are not. (Inferred regulatory mapping; the brief does not name frameworks, but banking-app password reset cannot exist outside this envelope.)

Audience-reach composition for a U.S. retail banking app is wider than a typical consumer-app default and filters direction shapes accordingly. The reset cohort skews disproportionately toward users who are infrequent app-users (forgot the password precisely because they don't open the app often — older adults, lower-digital-fluency users, users on older devices, users whose primary banking channel is still branch or phone), users in financial precarity (the rent-due framing), and users with a damaged trust relationship with the institution from the prior hostile flow. Directions that assume a fluent, recent, trusting user — biometric-only flows that quietly fail on a device the user just got, magic-link flows that assume an email inbox the user can reach from a locked phone, in-app-only flows that assume the user is already inside the very app they cannot get into — fail to reach a non-trivial share of the actual reset population. (Inferred from the brief's framing of the current flow as failing "people who need in NOW" rather than a power-user segment.)

A credible escalation path is itself a strategic filter, not an execution detail. The brief explicitly calls out the absence of escalation as part of what makes today's flow hostile. Directions that treat the automated reset as a closed system — succeed-or-bounce — are not viable for this brief; the viability bar requires that every direction propose where a user who cannot complete the automated path lands (human agent, branch, callback queue, alternate channel) and that the escalation is reachable from inside the failed flow, not buried in a separate support tree. Tesler's conservation-of-complexity applies at the strategic level: identity-proofing complexity is irreducible, so any direction that appears to simplify the user-facing flow is implicitly relocating the proof burden somewhere — onto a device-binding assumption, onto a support channel, onto a deferred re-verification, onto the institution's fraud-loss budget. Where the complexity lands is a direction-shaping decision, not an execution detail, and directions that do not name where it lands are not yet viable proposals.

Dimensions open for exploration rather than fixed: the specific mix of identity-proof factors (knowledge, possession, biometric, behavioral, trusted-device, third-party IdP, in-branch fallback) is a hypothesis space, not a constraint — the brief does not prescribe which factors to use, only that whatever combination is chosen must clear the regulatory floor and reach the actual audience composition under acute stress. The handoff geometry between automated and human channels (when, how, with what state preserved) is similarly open. The pre-auth entry surface — landing page, app cold-start, deep-linked SMS, search-result — is open and should be treated as part of the proposal, since reset begins outside the authenticated app.
